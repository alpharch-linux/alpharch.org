The desk assembles itself, and it reads the tape with you.

We are building an operating system with one customer in mind: the trader who reads order flow and options flow, and who is tired of spending the best minutes of the morning launching software.

Every trading session has the same shape. The same charts in the same places, the same execution platform, the same research, at the same times, every day. Alpharch treats that shape as the operating system's job. By 7:30 each morning it has already written your desk sheet, with the overnight session, the key levels, the dealer positioning map and your plan drawn from your own playbook. At 8:00, one keypress raises the entire desk: footprints on the first screen, execution on the second, research on the third. When the bell rings you are reading the market, not arranging windows.

It is built as a fork of Omarchy, the most consequential Linux desktop project in years, and it is being designed in the open, blueprint first. The install command below is honest about its status: the desk you see on this page exists as a complete working specification, now being built.

II.The flagship

It has read every tick.
Ask it anything.

No one can watch the footprint, the order book, the options tape and the internals at once. The Narrator can, and does, all session long.

It sits on the same data streams as every chart on the desk: each print, each iceberg, each pulled bid, each sweep and block, the live gamma map, the internals, your marked levels, and your own playbook file. When you ask, it answers out loud, in seconds, in your vocabulary. Your hands never leave the ladder. No charting platform offers this. No retail terminal offers this. It boots with the operating system.

You, by voice · 10:07:12
"Flow check."
The Narrator · 10:07:14
"Last twelve minutes: two absorption events at 6428, the overnight high held both times. CVD is diverging on this push. Zero-DTE call volume is building at 6440 and the wall has migrated up. TICK averaging +420, bonds bid, NQ leading. Your A-setup zone sits five points below."
You · 10:07:39
"Confluence check on the long."
The Narrator · 10:07:41
"Three of four supportive: bonds bid, NQ leading, VIX fading; the dollar is flat. Per your playbook, A-grade requires three or more. It qualifies. Sizing and trigger are yours."
Quiet by default
It speaks in three cases only: when you ask; when a watch you armed fires ("tell me if 6390 gets tested," and you hear how it traded, absorbed or sliced); or in narrate mode, for a stretch you choose, after which it goes silent again.
Your vocabulary, not commentary
It reads your playbook file, so it answers in your setups' names and restates your rules at the moment they apply. Voice runs locally in both directions.
Structurally unable to trade
Read-only streams and no path to the order ticket. It describes; it never advises. A tape-reading assistant that makes calls is a signal service with your account attached, and the Narrator is built so it cannot become one.

IV.The machinery

Futures first. Flow underneath everything.

The market, two keystrokes away.A switcher opens on the futures catalog grouped by exchange: CME equity index, CBOT rates and grains, NYMEX energy, COMEX metals. Front months resolve automatically, days-to-roll sit beside every contract, and micros are one keystroke from their full-size siblings.

Order flow as the default view.Footprints, cumulative delta and volume profile form the standard chart workspace, through MotiveWave running natively or Sierra Chart under a tested compatibility layer. The system also watches the tape itself: large-lot prints, icebergs, pulled liquidity, absorption, and delta divergence, raised as alerts tied to your marked levels.

A living gamma map.The options-flow engine computes the call wall, put wall and gamma flip before the open, then keeps them alive intraday as zero-DTE volume rebuilds the landscape, with an estimate of forced dealer hedging when a level breaks.

A desk that remembers.Every fill is journaled automatically with its full market context, and the analytics answer questions worth asking: which setups pay, at which times, under which conditions. Daily loss limits are enforced by the operating system itself.

VI.Provenance

Standing on the biggest thing to hit Linux in years.

A word on the foundation, for readers who don't follow the Linux world. In 2025, David Heinemeier Hansson, the programmer behind Ruby on Rails and Basecamp, released Omarchy: a finished, opinionated desktop built on Arch Linux that compressed weeks of expert configuration into a twenty-minute install, and made an AI assistant part of the operating system itself. It became the fastest-moving project in desktop Linux and is now backed by its own foundation.

This matters to a trader for one reason. The hard engineering underneath Alpharch — the installer, the update pipeline, the theming system, the AI plumbing — belongs to that well-funded upstream project and its team. We build the one thing they never will: the trading desk on top. The foundation has an organization behind it. The desk has a single purpose. You get both.

VII.Plain statements

What we will not pretend.

  1. On latency: retail order flow travels hundreds of milliseconds to a broker, and no kernel patch changes that. What ships instead is what matters at that distance: truthful clocks, a machine that never sleeps mid-session, and a power-failure plan.
  2. On Windows-only platforms: some trading software will simply never run on Linux. IBKR runs natively here, TradingView runs accelerated, and Sierra Chart runs well under a tested compatibility layer — but Quantower, NinjaTrader and their class stay on Windows, and we say so here rather than in a footnote.
  3. On data: real depth and live options flow are paid feeds, and the free tier is honest about being an approximation rather than dressing it up.
  4. On tilt: the profit-and-loss readout ships disabled. Watching unrealized P&L all day is how accounts die; turning it on is a decision, not a default.
  5. On the assistant: it analyzes everything and advises on nothing. No orders, no signals, no exceptions, by architecture rather than by policy.
